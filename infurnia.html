UX restructure announcement

Challenges

Being the sole designer

  • Fielded ad hoc requests from every team, with no shared queue or prioritisation system
  • Sat in on every cross-functional meeting to keep design context intact
  • Ran several parallel projects at once, with no dedicated bandwidth per initiative
  • Covered research, UX, visual design, and parts of the PM role at the same time

Operating in a developer-led org

  • Introduced a design process into a workflow that had none
  • Design was frequently deprioritised under delivery pressure
  • Engineering sometimes shipped a first pass before looping design in, given limited design bandwidth
  • Minor features and hotfixes went out without design review
  • UI/UX issues weren't consistently treated as release blockers

Working with an early-career team

  • The entire front-end team was early-career, which made pixel-accurate execution harder to guarantee
  • Absorbed most PM responsibilities for a full year in the absence of a dedicated PM
  • Ran a one-week sprint cadence set directly by the CEO
  • Worked alongside inexperienced PMs, which meant thinner product planning upstream

Owning requirements definition

  • PMs didn't produce PRDs, so specs often started as word-of-mouth problem briefs
  • Took ownership of drafting the PRD myself for most features
  • Identified user needs and scoped new features independently

Limited mentorship and feedback loops

  • No co-designers meant no peer review — learning was almost entirely self-directed
  • Sourced design feedback from a wide range of informal channels
  • Held frequent 1:1s with the CTO as the primary design-review loop

Other challenges

  • Carried PM responsibilities solo for a full year before a PM was hired
  • Built the design system from the ground up
  • Designed for 3D interfaces, a first for the team
  • Reconciled mental models across AutoCAD, SketchUp, and Revit into one coherent product

Things I enjoyed

Ownership

  • End-to-end ownership from problem definition to shipped UI, with no hand-offs or approval bottlenecks
  • Freedom to set the design process and standards for the org from scratch
  • Directly accountable for outcomes, not just deliverables

Direct access to leadership

  • Frequent, direct design reviews with the CTO meant fast decisions and real influence on product direction
  • No long approval chains — a good case for a change was usually enough to ship it
  • Close proximity to company strategy shaped how I prioritised design work
