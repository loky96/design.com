Game Description

Gameplay & Level Design

The core game loop is all about surviving the enemy in each round, It was hard to create entertaining levels with such a small game concept, I had to do lot of brain storming to create various levels for this games.

Made up to 80 levels, grouped them in pairs of 8 which deserves specific game play style.

Level Design Document

Legend: S = speed (speed1, speed2, speed3, varying speed) · P = points type · F = frequency (1=normal, 2=switch, 3=move, 4=push to score) · T = trigger Range (dist1, dist2, dist3)

After several play-testing session, I adjusted the difficulty of the level according to the given graph below. by following Csikzentmihalyi's flow state principle.

When person's ability to execute a task balances the difficulty of the task, they enter into the flow state, where people enjoy the game.

Took several game play test to find out the optimum speed and behaviours of the enemies to keep the flow state intact. Few screenshot of diffent levels are shows below

Defining Test Protocols

01

Functionality testing

Objective: Smooth onboarding — can users find and navigate the game's elements?

Goals given to each user

  1. Find the music controls
  2. Start the game and clear Level 1
  3. Clear Level 2, then Level 3
  4. Return to the Home screen
  5. Replay an earlier level
  6. Buy 500 diamonds
  7. Push through to Level 6 and head back home
02

Retrospective testing

Objective: Do players intuitively read the game's elements, and how does the play experience feel overall?

Goal 1
Clear Level 1
  • How did you clear it, and how did it feel?
  • Could you read the enemy's behavior?
  • How did you dodge it?
  • Did you clock what the sparkling thing was?
Goal 2
A few levels back to back
  • Can you read the enemy's behavior now?
  • What was the red thing about?
  • How does life work here?
  • How are star ratings earned?
Goal 3
Reading the difficulty curve
  • How does difficulty shift as levels climb?
  • Could you feel the music change level to level?

Music & Sound

  1. SFX vs. Master volume — nobody could tell them apart
  2. Mute/unmute itself was easy to find
  3. But the mute icon read ambiguous as a toggle
  4. Only a few players noticed the music shift between levels

Mental Model

Main menu
  • 2-player button sitting under Play read as ambiguous — mode switch, or something else?
  • Play's destination was unclear: level select, or straight into the game?
Settings
  • Referral claim was found by accident, if at all
Level description
  • Most players read it fully, the rest at least skimmed it
  • The Back button's destination — previous level or home — stayed a mystery
Game screen
  • Player control clicked instantly — everyone cleared Level 1
  • The heart read as "life," but its function stayed fuzzy until it ran out
  • The pause button was hard to find
  • Enemy behavior changes went unnoticed unless we pointed them out
Level complete
  • Stars read as a score, but how they're earned stayed unclear
  • The star button next to Play sparked wild guesses — leaderboard? premium? level select?
  • Share button: sharing the score, or the app itself?
Game over
  • Revive and the "3 lives" concept confused most players
  • The life icon on Retry — does it cost a life, or grant one?
  • Tapping Home didn't actually go home — a broken expectation
Diamonds
  • Mixed up with in-game points by some players
  • Their role on the level-complete screen wasn't clear

Retrospective Insights

01

First level

  • Play clicked intuitively for most; the levels felt fun and easy to read
"The instructions were short and crisp." / "Can't wait to click play."
02

A few levels in

  • By level 2, nobody needed the instructions anymore
  • "Game life" only clicked after an actual enemy encounter
"It takes just 5 sec to understand the enemy's behaviour, even if I miss the description."
03

Reading the difficulty curve

  • 10 of 12 players correctly tracked how difficulty escalated level by level
  • Variety in enemy behavior landed better than a flat difficulty ramp
"Especially in Level 4 — the enemy got faster and started moving randomly instead of chasing me!"

Our Takeaways

01

Should've asked for reactions after the fact, not mid-play — first instinct beats an interrupted one.

02

Players acted naturally until asked to explain themselves — then hesitated putting it into words.

Conclusion

01

Some buttons didn't read intuitively — but players adapted fast after one encounter.

02

Several elements only registered once we probed for them directly.

03

Level Select ↔ Home navigation was the single most reported friction point.

04

Metaphors (heart, diamonds, stars) were recognized — their function wasn't.

05

Functional gaps and all, players still enjoyed the game — that came first for them.
